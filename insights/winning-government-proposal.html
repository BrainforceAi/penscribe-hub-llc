Government contracting guide

What government proposal evaluators actually reward

A practical guide to writing compliant, evaluator-focused government proposals that connect requirements, proof, benefits, and measurable outcomes.

Winning government work starts before proposal writing. The strongest contractors qualify opportunities carefully, align their evidence to the buyer’s priorities, and make evaluation easy.

Compliance comes first

Create a compliance matrix that traces every instruction, requirement, form, attachment, and evaluation factor to its exact response location.

Write to the evaluation criteria

Mirror the buyer’s language and sequence. Make it effortless for evaluators to find the evidence required to award points.

Turn features into outcomes

Do not stop at describing your process. Explain the resulting schedule reliability, cost control, quality, safety, or mission continuity.

Use proof, not adjectives

Replace claims such as “highly experienced” with contract values, performance periods, volumes, response times, quality scores, and client outcomes.

Make risk visible—and controlled

Name the operational risks the agency cares about, then show preventive controls, owners, triggers, and corrective actions.

Review like an evaluator

Use a final red-team review that scores the response against published factors, checks every requirement, and removes unsupported claims.
