Government contracting guide

SAM.gov is only the beginning

SAM.gov registration is only one step. Learn what small businesses need to become visible, credible, and ready to compete for federal contracts.

Winning government work starts before proposal writing. The strongest contractors qualify opportunities carefully, align their evidence to the buyer’s priorities, and make evaluation easy.

Complete the registration correctly

Your legal entity, tax records, banking information, NAICS codes, CAGE information, and representations must align. Inconsistencies create delays.

Build a buyer-focused capability statement

Lead with core competencies, differentiators, relevant performance, codes, certifications, and a direct point of contact.

Choose a focused market

A long list of NAICS codes is not a strategy. Select agencies and buying offices with recurring demand that fits your proven capacity.

Create past-performance assets

Prepare concise project summaries with scope, size, complexity, outcomes, and references before a live opportunity arrives.

Monitor the right signals

Track forecasts, procurement histories, sources sought, RFIs, expiring contracts, and agency small-business goals - not only open solicitations.

Start relationships before the RFP

Introduce your firm to small-business specialists, program offices, primes, and potential teaming partners while requirements are still forming.
